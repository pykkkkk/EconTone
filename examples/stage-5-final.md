This paper asks whether minimum wage increases are associated with youth employment in China, using
provincial panel data from 2004 to 2019. Our baseline is a two-way fixed-effects model with province and year
fixed effects, and standard errors are clustered at the province level. The association is negative and larger
for low-skilled workers, at about [MAGNITUDE?] percentage points. We do not claim identification: provinces
adjust minimum wages in response to local conditions, and youth employment is measured from a rotating survey.
The estimates therefore describe an association, not a causal effect of the reform. Whether the pattern
reflects substitution toward capital is a question these data cannot answer.
