In order to investigate the impact of minimum wage increases on youth employment in China, this paper utilizes provincial panel data from 2004 to 2019. We employ a fixed-effects model and find that minimum wage increases significantly reduce youth employment. It is worth noting that the effect is more pronounced for low-skilled workers. The mechanism is that firms substitute capital for labor when labor becomes more expensive. In addition, we conduct a series of robustness checks which confirm our findings. These results underscore the importance of flexible labor markets and offer important policy implications for developing countries, highlighting the transformative potential of minimum wage reform for inclusive growth.
